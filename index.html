Phase 1

Needs Assessment

What learners told us, and the need we chose to address.

Identified problem

Junior High and Senior High School learners at the USLS Basic Education Unit use devices almost all day. Many are distracted from studying, have seen scams or unsafe content, and are not confident they can protect their personal information. Our priority need is balanced screen time and online safety.

Who we helped

Selected Junior High School and Senior High School learners of the Basic Education Unit (BEU), University of St. La Salle. Parents and teachers can also benefit.

How we found out

A 10-question anonymous Google Form answered by 34 BEU learners.

Summary of data

Key results

Each block is one learner. 34 blocks per row.

Learners per answer

Each block is one learner. The number is how many learners picked that answer.

Source: Group 6 needs assessment survey, BEU learners, n = 34. Percentages are rounded to whole numbers. "Rarely or never" and "No" are the learners left after the other answers.

Key findings

  • All 34 learners use a device every day (28) or almost every day (6). 30 of 34 use devices more than 4 hours a day.
  • 30 of 34 are distracted from studying often (15) or sometimes (15).
  • 30 of 34 have seen scams, suspicious links, fake accounts, or unsafe content: 19 often and 11 sometimes.
  • Only 8 of 34 are "very sure" they can keep their personal information safe. 10 of 34 said they only "somewhat" know how to make a strong password.
  • 32 of 34 would join a hands-on Expo (16 yes and 16 maybe).

The three stations

Pause

Spot the Scam

Sort six sample messages, links, and accounts into Safe or Scam. Four red flags: hurry, too good to be true, asks for private info, strange sender or link.

Protect

Password Power-Up

Use a simple sentence method to build a strong sample password: long, mixed, unique, and private. Never a real password.

Post

Screen Time Check

Open your phone's screen time feature, look at your daily average, and write one realistic goal on a pledge card for the Pledge Wall.

Main points

The community issue

Learners are online for hours every day, and most have seen scams or unsafe content.

The problem

Distractions, scams, weak passwords, oversharing, and cyberbullying.

The topic we shared

Pause (spot scams), Protect (passwords and privacy), Post (screen time and being a good digital citizen).

Proposed solutions

Three habits: Pause before you tap, Protect what is yours, Post with purpose.

Role of science and technology

Screen time tools, two-step verification, and report buttons help. Endless scroll and scammers who copy real brands harm. Laws, schools, and families shape how tools are used.

Expected impact

Learners who can spot scams, build stronger passwords, and set their own goals, and who share these habits with friends and family.

Games inside the session

Spot the Scam

Six cards to sort into Safe or Scam, with the red flags explained after each one.

Password Power-Up

Turn a sentence into a password and see a live strength check. Nothing is saved or sent.

Screen Time Check

Enter your daily hours, see what it adds up to, and write a pledge.

Phase 5

SDG Connection

How our project supports the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals.

Related SDGs

4

SDG 4: Quality Education

Main goal. Target 4.4 is about more young people having skills they need. Its indicator 4.4.1 tracks youth and adults with ICT (information and communications technology) skills.

3

SDG 3: Good Health and Well-being

Target 3.4 includes promoting mental health and well-being.

16

SDG 16: Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions

Target 16.2 aims to end abuse, exploitation, and violence against children.

Problem addressed

Learners spend long hours online, are often distracted, meet scams and unsafe content, and many are unsure how to protect their information. This affects their learning, their health, and their safety.

How the problem connects to each goal

SDG 4

Safe, balanced technology use is a basic skill for learning today. Our stations teach it by practice.

SDG 3

Screen time can crowd out sleep, movement, and time with people. Our Screen Time Check builds healthier habits.

SDG 16

Cyberbullying and online exploitation harm young people. Learners practice what to do: save proof, block, report, and tell a trusted adult.

Impact of our project

Individuals

A learner who knows the four red flags can stop before tapping a fake link. A sentence-based password is stronger than a name plus a birth year. A pledge like "No phone during homework for 1 hour" protects focus and sleep.

Community

Learners share what they learn with friends, family, and group chats. Teachers and parents get a shared vocabulary. Paper-based stations let learners without devices or stable internet take part.

Environment

The session runs mostly on a free website, and the booth cards and poster are reused for every group.

Individual reflection

Our Reflections

As a Lasallian student, what values or learning have you gained from this project, and how have these helped you recognize and use your gifts in service of others?

Navisaga, Vanessa DayneGROUP LEADER

Leader · coordinated the team and built the interactive website

As group leader, my job was to keep six people moving in the same direction. I set our weekly tasks, followed up on deadlines, and put our work together, including the interactive website for our session. The hardest part was fitting everyone's schedules, and I learned that leading means listening first and deciding second.

This project also showed me that technology is not good or bad by itself. The same phone that distracts 30 of the 34 learners we surveyed can also teach them to spot a scam. Communion in Mission and Service guided me most, because we only finished by letting each member use a different strength, and because our work was for learners and not only for a grade.

My gift is organizing people and ideas, and I now see it as a way to serve. After this project I want to keep using my coding skills to build tools that help my school community.

Fronda, Ingge Lance

Survey and data team

I helped write the survey and read the results. Writing 10 questions sounds easy until each one has to be short, clear, and fair. We had to rewrite several so learners would not feel pushed toward an answer. That taught me to respect the people who answer our questions.

The numbers changed how I saw the project. Learning that 30 of 34 learners had seen scams or unsafe content turned a topic into real classmates who needed help. This is what Faith means to me: looking at the evidence honestly and letting it guide what we do.

My gift is patience with details, and I used it to check our counts and percentages. I learned that careful work is also a form of service, because other people trust the numbers we give them.

Jaluag, Myke Loren

Station team · Spot the Scam

I prepared to facilitate the Spot the Scam station. To explain scams well, I had to understand them. I learned that scammers do not only use tricks with technology. They use feelings: fear, excitement, trust, and hurry. That is why the first rule we teach is to pause.

Respect was my main value. A learner who falls for a scam is not foolish, so I practiced explaining the warning signs without blaming anyone. Zeal helped me too, because I kept rewriting the sample cards until they looked like messages learners really receive.

My gift is explaining things in a way people understand. This project showed me that I can use it to protect others, and I want to keep sharing what I know with my family and friends.

Sebua, David Joshua

Design team · poster and printed materials

I worked on the poster and printed materials. I learned that design is part of accessibility. If the text is too small or the colors are confusing, the message does not reach the learner, no matter how good it is. We kept the words short and the colors consistent for each station.

Zeal was the value I felt most. It meant putting in extra effort so that learning looks engaging, not boring. Communion in Mission was just as important, since the design only worked when the station team told me what learners needed to see.

My gift is visual thinking. I used to see it as a hobby, but now I see it as a way to serve, because a clear poster can help a learner stay safe online. I want to keep using it for school and community projects.

Sinadjan, Rojin Andrea

Station team · Password Power-Up

I prepared the Password Power-Up station. I learned that a strong password does not have to be hard to remember. If you turn a sentence only you would think of into a password, it becomes long, mixed, and easy to recall. I also learned that 10 of the 34 learners we surveyed only "somewhat" knew how to make one.

Respect shaped how we ran the station. Learners build a sample password on paper and never use a real one, so nobody has to share anything private. Service means giving people a skill they can use every day, not just telling them what to do.

My gift is turning technical ideas into simple steps. This project helped me see that this gift can protect other people, and I want to keep using it when friends or family ask me for help with their accounts.

Tepino, Franchezka Jazz

Station team · Screen Time Check and Pledge Wall

I prepared the Screen Time Check station and the Pledge Wall. While getting ready, I checked my own screen time and was surprised by how many hours it showed. That made me understand why 30 of the 34 learners we surveyed said they are distracted from studying. It is easy to lose track of time.

I learned that a goal works better when it is small and realistic, such as no phone during homework for one hour. Faith, for me, is reflecting honestly on my own habits before asking others to change theirs. Service is helping without judging.

My gift is listening and encouraging people. This project taught me that these are real strengths in service. I plan to keep my own screen time goal and encourage the people around me to try one.
